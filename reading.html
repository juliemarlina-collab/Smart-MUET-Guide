2
The SCAN Technique
A four-step method for every question
▾
SStudy the question
CCircle keywords
AAnalyse & scan
NNarrow the choices
S — Study the question first.
Identify what the question is asking: a fact, main idea, inference, purpose, vocabulary meaning or reference.
C — Circle the keywords.
Mark names, dates, numbers and strong content words. Think of possible synonyms because the passage may paraphrase them.
A — Analyse and scan the passage.
Move your eyes quickly to locate the matching section. Read one or two sentences before and after the keyword for context.
N — Narrow the choices.
Eliminate options that are not mentioned, partly true, too general or opposite in meaning. Choose the option supported by evidence.
GOLDEN RULE

Match the meaning, not only the same words. A distractor may copy words from the passage but change the idea.

Question Types & Test Parts

Common question types

Main IdeaSpecific DetailInference Vocabulary in ContextWriter's PurposeTone & AttitudeReferenceCohesion
Parts 1–2Short notices, advertisements, messages and everyday texts. Focus on direct information and purpose.
Parts 3–4Longer factual or narrative texts. Track main ideas, details, sequence and paraphrases.
Part 5Gapped text or cohesion task. Follow pronouns, linkers, repeated ideas and paragraph flow.
Parts 6–7Extended academic texts. Handle inference, writer's view, purpose, tone and evaluation.

Fast clue guide

“According to the text…” → locate a direct detail.
“It can be inferred…” → combine clues; do not add outside knowledge.
“The writer's purpose…” → decide whether the writer informs, explains, argues, warns or persuades.
“The word/phrase refers to…” → look backwards for the nearest logical noun or idea.
4
Worked Example
Apply SCAN step by step
▾
Sample passage: Despite the increasing availability of electric vehicles, many Malaysians still prefer conventional cars because they are concerned about charging infrastructure. A survey found that 68% identified limited charging stations as the main barrier.

Question: What is the main reason Malaysians hesitate to buy electric vehicles?

S — STUDY
The question asks for the main reason for hesitation.
C — CIRCLE
Keywords: main reason, Malaysians, hesitate, electric vehicles.
A — ANALYSE
The passage says “limited charging stations” are the “main barrier.” This is the evidence sentence.
N — NARROW
Option A paraphrases the evidence. Options B and C are not stated, so eliminate them.
Correct answer: A. “Limited” is paraphrased as “insufficient,” while “main barrier” matches “main reason.”
5
Exam Workflow & Quick Reference
Timing, traps and final checks
▾
75-MINUTE WORKFLOW

First pass: answer easier direct questions. Second pass: return to inference and difficult items. Reserve the final minutes to check unanswered questions.

⏱ Pace yourselfAim for about 1½ minutes per question, but spend less time on short-text items.
🚩 Mark and moveDo not allow one difficult question to consume several minutes.
🧩 Use text evidenceYour answer must be supported by the passage, not personal experience.
✅ Answer every itemThere is no benefit in leaving a multiple-choice question blank.

Common traps to avoid

❌ Choosing an option only because it repeats words from the passage.
❌ Using outside knowledge instead of the information given.
❌ Reading the entire passage slowly before looking at the questions.
❌ Ignoring words such as not, except, mainly and most likely.
BEFORE YOU SUBMIT

Check that every question has one answer, difficult items were reviewed and your final choice matches evidence from the text.
