Before You Start
What Speaking 800/2 tests
▾

What MUET Speaking Tests

  • Task A — Individual response. You respond to a question alone for 2 minutes (1 min prep + 1–2 min speaking).
  • Task B — Group discussion. You discuss a topic with 3–4 candidates for 10 minutes. The group must reach a consensus.
  • Both tasks test: task fulfilment, idea development, language control, fluency and delivery.
  • Each task is worth 45 marks. Total: 90 marks (25% of MUET).
2
Tips & Tricks
Strategies before you practise
▾
TASK A STRATEGY

Always use PREP: Point → Reason → Example → Point again. Write 3–4 keywords during prep time, not full sentences. Start speaking the moment prep time ends — do not wait.

TASK B STRATEGY

Task B is a group discussion, not a lecture. Refer to other candidates by name. Ask questions: "What do you think, Aisyah?". Help the group reach a consensus in the final minute.

Top 5 Speaking Tips

  1. State your main point in the first sentence. Examiners decide quickly.
  2. Give one strong reason, not three weak ones.
  3. Use a specific example — Malaysian context works well (e.g., PTPTN, EPU, JPA).
  4. Avoid fillers: um, uh, like, you know. Pause briefly instead.
  5. Close by restating your point — this is the second P in PREP.

Vault 1 Task A — Sample Question

Your task: Talk about SALARY as an important reason when choosing a job. (1 minute preparation, 1–2 minutes speaking)

Sample PREP Response Band 4.0

P — Point I believe salary is the most important reason when choosing a job because it directly determines a person's financial security and quality of life.
R — Reason A good salary allows a fresh graduate to repay education loans, cover daily expenses and begin saving for the future — all at the same time. Without a sufficient salary, other aspects of the job become secondary.
E — Example For example, many Malaysian graduates with PTPTN loans prioritise salary when job hunting because repayment begins six months after graduation. A starting salary below RM2,000 in a high cost-of-living city like Kuala Lumpur makes loan repayment extremely difficult alongside rent and transport costs.
P — Point Again Therefore, I firmly believe that salary is the most critical factor when selecting a career, as it forms the foundation of an individual's financial independence and long-term stability.
EXAMINER NOTE

This response is direct, uses one strong reason, gives a specific local example (PTPTN, KL cost of living) and closes with a clear restatement. Approximately 90 seconds. No filler words.

Task B — Sample Interaction Phrases

Topic: How universities can provide better career guidance for students

I think internship programmes are the most effective because they give students real workplace experience. What do you think, [name]?
That is a good point. I would like to add that career guidance advisers could personalise the support for each student.
Can we agree that the most practical solution for most universities would be internship programmes, since they directly connect students with employers?
I think we should conclude that internship programmes are the best way, as most of us agree it provides both exposure and real skills.
